# Drift grade standard

## The job's goal

Every piece of marketing work the company puts out is on the approved
version of its positioning, its customer profile, its messaging and its
voice. When the work and the base disagree, the team finds out which one is
behind before the piece goes out.

## What is unacceptable

- A claim the positioning or the messaging no longer carries.
- A claim no document in the base supports.
- A comparison with a competitor that the base does not support.
- A piece written for an audience the customer profile does not name.
- A line that breaks the voice guide, when the base holds one.

## What needs approval

Any change to a document in the base. The grade never changes one. When the
work carries evidence the base does not hold, the grade proposes the change,
and the document's owner decides whether to accept it.

## Checks before work is handed back

- Every finding quotes the line in the piece and the line in the base it was
  judged against, with that document's path.
- Every document a finding cites is one the grade was given.
- Every optional document the base does not hold is named as a gap.
- The grade says it changed nothing in the base, and it did not.

## How each threshold relates to a goal

This job sets no threshold and serves no goal with a number. The verdict is
about the piece as measured against the base as it stands today. When a
document is flagged because a recorded context change has
overtaken it, the grade waits for the person's choice about that document
first, because a grade against an overtaken document would be measured
against the wrong version.
