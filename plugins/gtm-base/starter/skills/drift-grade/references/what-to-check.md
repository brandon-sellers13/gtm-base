# What to check

Each kind of finding, what counts as one, and what does not. The standard
decides what is unacceptable; this says how to recognise it in a line.

## A claim the base no longer carries

It counts when the piece says something the positioning or the messaging
used to say and now says it stopped saying, or says differently. Quote the
line in the document that says what changed.

It does not count when the piece puts an approved claim in other words. Two
sentences that mean the same thing are on the approved version.

## A claim with no source in the base

It counts when the piece states a capability, a result or a promise that no
document you were given supports.

It does not count when the claim rests on a document the base does not hold.
Then name that document under the gaps instead, because you cannot tell
whether the claim is right.

## A competitor comparison the base does not support

It counts when the piece compares the company with another one by name, or
with a kind of product, in a way the positioning or the competitors document
does not say. Without a competitors document, judge only against the
positioning, and name the missing document under the gaps.

## An audience the profile does not name

It counts when the piece is written for a reader the customer profile does
not describe: another kind of company, another role, another size of
business. Quote the profile's line that says who the customer is.

When several lines speak to the same new reader, and the piece carries
evidence that reader is real, such as a customer's own words, this is the
case for a proposed context change rather than a list of fixes.

## How the piece sounds

Only with a voice guide. It counts when a line breaks one of the guide's
principles or uses a word the guide says to avoid. Quote the principle or
the word. Without a voice guide, say nothing about how the piece sounds.
