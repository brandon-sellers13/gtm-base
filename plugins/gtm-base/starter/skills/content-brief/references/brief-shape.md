# The shape of a brief

What each part is for, and what a writer does with it.

## Audience

The reader the piece is written for, in the words the base uses for them. A
segment's own document is more specific than the profile, so when the person
names a segment and the base holds its document, its words win. Say which
document the audience came from, so the writer can read it in full.

## The one claim and its proof

A piece that makes three claims is remembered for none. The brief names one,
quoted from the messaging, or from the positioning when the base holds no
messaging, and the proof the messaging gives for it. A claim with no proof in
the base is still the claim; its proof is then a gap.

## Speaks to

The buyer persona the piece is for, from the buyer personas: their role, what
they are measured on, and the objection they raise most. Without the buyer
personas this is a gap.

## Voice for this channel

The voice guide's principles and words that matter most for this channel,
quoted. Never describe a voice the guide does not hold; a generic voice such as
"friendly and professional" is worse than the gap.

## Looks

What the design guide says any piece must respect, such as the forbidden
devices. Without a design guide this is a gap.

## Gaps

Every document the brief needed and the base does not hold, by name. The
writer reads this first, because it says where they will have to ask.
