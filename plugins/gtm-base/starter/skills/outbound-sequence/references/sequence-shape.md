# The shape of a sequence

## How many emails, and how long

The standard sets how many emails a sequence holds and how long each may be.
When it says nothing, write a short sequence of plain emails, each short
enough to read on a phone without scrolling.

## Where the words come from

The segment's own document is the first place to look for how its buyers
describe their problem, and its words go into the emails as they are. The
positioning says what the company is. The messaging gives the claims and the
proof. The voice guide says how it sounds. The competitors say what the
buyer uses today, which is often the best opening line.

## The claims table

Every claim an email makes is a row: the claim as the email makes it, and the
path of the document behind it. A greeting, a question or a sign-off is not a
claim. A claim that rests on nothing in the base is not written into an email
at all; it goes under "Dropped", quoted, so the person can see what was left
out and why.

## What never goes in

No prospect's name, email address or phone number, because the base holds
none and a sequence is written to a role. No figure the base does not carry.
No claim about a competitor the base does not make.
